Distinguishing Kolhapur RTO (MH-09), Ichalkaranji RTO (MH-51), District & Region

Per the Maharashtra Transport Department Statistics Information 2024–25 report, Kolhapur RTO (MH-09) recorded 60,246 new registrations and a total registered vehicle population of 1,842,263 as of 31 March 2025. Ichalkaranji RTO (MH-51) is a separate office with 19,748 new registrations, while the Kolhapur Region aggregate (Kolhapur, Sangli, Satara, Karad, Ichalkaranji) recorded 204,486 new registrations. Important Caveat: The ratio $15,044 / 60,246 = 24.97\%$ must not be published as a definitive EV penetration rate because the $15,044$ EV numerator (reported by ET Auto / Times of India on 4 April 2025) reflects district-level reporting while $60,246$ is the MH-09 office denominator. B2B Partnership & Landowner Revenue Models

Model A: 100% CPO Capex + Landowner Revenue Share Zero Host CAPEX

Ideal for Colleges (Shivaji Univ, DYP), Malls (Reliance Mall, DYP City Mall), and Hospitals. The Charge Point Operator (CPO) invests 100% of hardware & MSEDCL meter CAPEX and pays the host institution a ₹1.50 to ₹2.50/kWh royalty (or 15%–20% net revenue share) instead of fixed land rent.

Model B: Co-Investment / 50:50 Joint Venture Fastest Payback

Ideal for NH-48 Highway Food Malls, Hotels (Sayaji, Vrishali, Opal, 7/12), and Fuel Stations (BPCL/IOCL/HPCL). Property owner funds civil/electrical works (~30% CAPEX) while CPO supplies charger & CMS software (~70% CAPEX), splitting net EBITDA 50:50.

Model C: 100% Host / Franchisee Owned (White-Label SaaS) 100% Host Profit

Ideal for Auto Dealerships (Shiroli MIDC, Kawala Naka), Co-op Housing Societies, and Fleet Operators. Host purchases the charger outright and pays only a 5%–8% OCPP CMS software fee for UPI QR billing, real-time app discovery, and remote diagnostics.

7. Overall Conclusion: Triangulating Official RTO Statistics, 47-Site + 37-Lead GIS Audits, Citizen Voice & Commercial Viability

By synthesizing official Maharashtra Transport Department RTO statistics (60,246 new MH-09 registrations and 1,842,263 total vehicles; 19,748 Ichalkaranji MH-51 registrations; 204,486 Kolhapur Region registrations; and 15,044 reported district EVs in FY 2024–25, up +45.89% YoY), a 47-station Master Directory + 27 ChargeDekho leads (L01–L27) + 10 Showroom & Hotel Sayaji corridor leads (N01–N10), 35 FAME-II scheme benchmarks, 39 verified citizen survey responses, and our 3-Tier Commercial ROI Model, this project establishes four conclusive findings:

1. The Vehicle-to-Charger Inversion (96.91% 2W vs. 19.15% 2W Stations): Electric two-wheelers account for 96.91% (14,579 of 15,044) of Kolhapur’s EV registrations and 89.74% (35 of 39) of surveyed users, yet 72.34% (34 of 47) of Master Directory stations—and virtually all newly discovered highway and dealership DC fast-charger leads—target 4-wheeler cars, forcing 79.49% of citizens to rely solely on home charging.
2. Spatial Inequality & Coordinate Verification Rigour: Only 8 of 47 Master stations possess exact published registry coordinates (BEE India, Open Charge Map, evgoo—including newly added #47 PowerUP / MSEDCL Shenda Park Substation at $16.67417^\circ\text{N}, 74.237358^\circ\text{E}$), while eastern NH-48 and Kawala Naka/Sayaji corridor clustering leaves western residential wards and campuses unserved. Consequently, 76.92% of citizens find locating a charger Difficult/Very Difficult.
3. Directory Inflation, Alias Duplication & Uptime Deficits: Our Showroom & Sayaji Corridor Recheck (N01–N10) proves why raw aggregator totals (38 to 105 points) cannot be taken at face value: multiple listings refer to the same physical site (e.g., E-Fill N01 & Vijaya Power N02 at DYP City Mall), report conflicting ratings (MAI Hyundai N03: 30 kW vs. 120 kW), or misclassify auto dealerships without public chargers (Mohite Suzuki N06). Combined with a 25% connector fault rate among audited fast chargers and unavailable status on Statiq ID 9184 for MSEDCL Shenda Park, this directly explains why 66.67% of surveyed citizens report queues, unavailable guns, or poor maintenance.
4. Commercial Viability & 100% Citizen Consensus: Every surveyed citizen (100% — 56.41% Definitely, 43.59% Probably) confirmed that better charging infrastructure will accelerate EV switching. Our financial engineering model proves that deploying Tier-1 2W Campus/Ward Micro-Hubs (₹3.20L CAPEX, 41.0% ROI, 9.93% breakeven utilization), Tier-2 Urban Hybrid Hubs (₹12.00L CAPEX, 44.9% ROI), and Tier-3 NH-48 Ultra-Fast Hubs (₹29.20L CAPEX, 55.7% ROI) achieves full payback in 21.5 to 29.3 months—making Kolhapur’s EV transition both socially urgent and commercially bankable.
